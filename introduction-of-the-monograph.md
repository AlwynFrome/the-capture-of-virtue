# Introduction

For many years, I have found myself unable to escape discussions concerning China. They have become an invisible wall—one that continues to surround me even after I have physically left the environment in which those discussions were once inescapable. Distance, it turns out, does not necessarily bring detachment. Many of the observations and reflections presented in this book originate from that persistent sense of intellectual unease. I cannot claim with confidence that they capture the truth, or even a fragment of it. At best, they represent an attempt to approach questions that have long resisted satisfactory answers. This raises a broader question about the nature of scholarship itself. Is academic writing ultimately undertaken in pursuit of truth, in response to emotion, or as an effort to confront personal and collective trauma? There may be no definitive answer. Yet the motivations behind an intellectual project do not, in themselves, determine the validity of its arguments. A work born from emotional experience may nevertheless illuminate realities that detached analysis alone cannot fully reveal. Perhaps this is one of the paradoxes of writing: its deepest significance often lies less in what it ultimately offers its readers than in what it enables its author to understand.

This book proceeds from the ordinary rather than the extraordinary. It begins with seemingly mundane details of everyday life and develops its arguments through a gradual process of logical inquiry, before ultimately stepping back to examine the broader contours of the question at hand. The conclusion, if one can call it that, is not predetermined but emerges only through this progression; It is in this spirit that I ask the reader to enter this inquiry alongside me.

For I cannot claim to know, at the outset, where it will ultimately lead. The destination remains uncertain even to its author. If the reflections collected here make a meaningful contribution to scholarship, I will consider myself deeply fortunate. If, instead, they prove to be little more than the expression of a personal intellectual sensibility, then they will nevertheless have allowed me to lay down a burden I have carried for many years. In either case, I regard transparency as essential. Throughout this work, I will identify, wherever possible, the origins of my ideas and the sources upon which they are based, so that readers may distinguish my own interpretations from the evidence on which they rest.

With that, let us begin.
